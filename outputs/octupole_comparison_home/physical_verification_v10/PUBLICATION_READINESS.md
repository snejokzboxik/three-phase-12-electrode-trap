# Publication readiness assessment

## Current assessment: B

The proposed `m=4×8` mechanism for an early standing-field escape has survived direct native P4 evaluation, h35/h50 comparison, strict integration tolerances, independent field-gradient checks, and a controlled relative-phase intervention. This is substantially stronger than the original N48-only observation.

The data do not support the broadest paper claims. Late escape times depend materially on FEM resolution; the h35 N48 classification at one positive boundary point disagrees with direct P4; the direct sample is deliberately small; the three-point energy/radius pilot is not a parameter map; the independent phase intervention is synthetic rather than an independently driven electrode protocol; and no chaos or infinite-time stability claim is supported.

## Narrow defensible claim

For the saved FEM field of this 12-electrode geometry and the stated matched-leading-`m=4` protocol, the directly measured opposite-helicity `m=4` and `m=8` components produce a large standing-field cross contribution to the leading pseudopotential near the aperture. In a controlled two-harmonic model, varying the relative temporal phase suppresses or restores the early finite-time escape for the selected launch. The same cross term is absent, to numerical precision, for the rotating two-harmonic field.

## Before submission

1. Spatially refine the near-boundary P4 outcomes or demonstrate a field-converged transition interval.
2. The compact energy/radius pilot has been completed at three matched h50 P4 starts. Expand only if the manuscript needs quantitative boundary widths; these points alone do not set thresholds.
3. Connect the synthetic harmonic phase change to an implementable electrode waveform, or state clearly that it is a causal model intervention.
4. Add direct P4 trajectory energy/exit-angle comparisons for both velocity directions and relevant phases.
5. Expand the literature comparison and check journal-specific novelty against the newly accepted rotating-RF trap work.
